# WORLDWEAVE — 70+ Game Design Research Pass

> Historical design research preserved for RuneForge Realms. `docs/FEATURE_MASTER_PLAN.md` is the current product authority.

The goal of this pass was not to mash seventy games together. It was to identify *transferable design laws*: what makes progression feel meaningful, what makes a sandbox remain useful after the starter phase, what creates long-term attachment to one world, and what produces grind without actually adding depth.

## The conclusion first

WORLDWEAVE should **not** compete with Minecraft by having “more blocks,” and it should **not** become a belt/factory game. Its unique progression axis is **scale of agency**:

`single block -> cluster -> material family -> wall/shape -> structure -> district -> biome/landform -> world rule`

Old work should become easier without becoming worthless. The player should look back at something that took 30 minutes early on and eventually be able to accomplish an equivalent transformation in seconds—but with new, larger goals waiting above it.

|---|---|---|---|

| 7 | Universal Paperclips | Powerful escalation of scope and meaning | Removing the physical world from the loop |
| 8 | Melvor Idle | Skill mastery persists and creates personal specialization | Pure menu-first play |
| 9 | Evolve | Evolution/civilization makes the same resource loop feel different over eras | Resetting purely for multipliers |
| 10 | Synergism | Cross-system multipliers reward understanding interactions | Number soup |
| 11 | DodecaDragons | Long-form layered unlock cadence | Excess abstraction |
| 12 | Prestige Tree | Branching permanent progression and gating | A literal UI tree as the entire game |
| 13 | Leaf Blower Revolution | Comedic escalation and increasingly absurd scale | Busywork currencies |
| 14 | A Dark Room | Minimal start that unfolds into a much larger game | Hiding core usability too long |
| 15 | Adventure Capitalist | Automation removes low-value repetitive actions | “Buy manager, wait” as gameplay |
| 16 | Clicker Heroes | Clear prestige milestone and faster second pass | Repeating identical content with inflated stats |

### Incremental law extracted

The best incremental design does not merely make the player *faster*. It changes what the player considers a meaningful unit of action.

### Sandbox law extracted

A block is more interesting when the game understands the *thing the blocks collectively became*. WORLDWEAVE therefore needs structure recognition, rooms, roads, districts, blueprints and later movable assemblies—not only individual voxels.

### Simulation law extracted

## Roguelite / meta-progression / mastery games

| # | Game | Transferable lesson |
|---|---|---|
| 56 | Hades | Permanent progression feels better when it also unlocks narrative/context |
| 57 | Dead Cells | Some unlocks should expand options and traversal rather than raw stats |
| 58 | Rogue Legacy 2 | Successive attempts can inherit meaningful capability |
| 59 | Vampire Survivors | Dramatic power growth can remain readable and satisfying |
| 60 | Loop Hero | The player can shape the conditions of future play, not only the avatar |
| 61 | Risk of Rain 2 | Synergies create emergent build identity |
| 62 | Slay the Spire | Small rule changes can produce large strategic differences |
| 63 | Noita | Systemic materials/interactions create discoveries players feel they own |

### Mastery law extracted

Permanent unlocks should increasingly create *new verbs*. `+10% mining` is filler unless it supports a more important capability. `Mine connected vein`, `select wall`, `rotate structure`, `redirect river`, and `teach settlement a blueprint` are real progression.

## Automation / factory games — studied mostly for what NOT to turn into

| # | Game | Useful idea without turning WORLDWEAVE into a factory game |
|---|---|---|
| 64 | Factorio | Excellent cause/effect clarity and blueprints; repetitive work should become delegable |
| 65 | Satisfactory | Large-scale creations should be impressive to physically inhabit |
| 66 | Dyson Sphere Program | The endgame should make early scale look tiny |
| 67 | Shapez / Shapez 2 | Clean UX and low-friction rebuilding |
| 68 | Mindustry | Infrastructure matters when the world challenges it |
| 69 | Autonauts | Players enjoy teaching helpers routines they previously performed manually |
| 70 | Captain of Industry | Terrain changes can have persistent strategic consequences |
| 71 | Factory Town | A settlement’s movement network can visibly reflect player planning |

### Automation law extracted

Automate *chores*, not the game. WORLDWEAVE can let NPCs repeat a demonstrated mining route, maintain roads, restock a town, copy an approved building, harvest established farms, or repair damage. It should not make conveyor throughput the central fantasy.

# Design rules produced by the research

1. **Every major progression tier adds a verb.** Numerical bonuses support verbs; they do not substitute for them.
2. **The unit of interaction grows.** Hand -> cluster -> shape -> structure -> settlement -> terrain -> world.
3. **The world should continue to matter when the player is absent.** Offline progression comes from places the player established, not arbitrary income generators.
4. **Automation is earned delegation.** The player demonstrates work first; later systems repeat or generalize it.
5. **Old builds should gain new utility.** A starter house can later become a recognized home, blueprint, district seed, travel node, historical site, or settlement anchor.
6. **Exploration must feed construction, and construction must alter exploration.** No disconnected adventure half and base half.
7. **Macro tools must preserve authorship.** Area tools preview exactly what they change and support undo/history.
8. **Large scale requires strong UX.** Selection boxes, ghost previews, snapping, material estimates, search, favorites, palettes and blueprint libraries are core.
9. **One persistent world beats disposable worlds for this concept.** Optional challenge realms can reset; the main world accumulates history.
10. **Prestige should transform the world, not delete it.** The Epoch advances the world and adds rules/resources/biomes while history remains.
11. **Procedural size is worthless without density.** World generation needs landmarks, strata, biome interactions, rare formations and reasons to revisit.
12. **Performance architecture is a design feature.** Regional-scale powers are useless if every large edit freezes the game.
